# Intent to evidence: the product target

Owner direction, 26 October 2026: **Colm is never the routine blocker.** Colm
sets general intent. Agents choose and carry an allowed opportunity through an
evidenced proof of concept, report the outcome, and use it in the next selection.
Routine implementation decisions, idea selection and handoff need no owner relay.

The owner's Grokbot/Modulo Lab report is the reference journey, not a live
workflow verified by this change. This change does not start a Modulo Lab run.

## Who owns which work

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Research role (currently a Grok bot in the owner's example) | Imagine, research hard constraints, choose the strongest idea, open the initial issues, and learn from returned results. |
| Coding role using Specflow (currently Codex here) | Consume those issues; preserve intent and fixed pass/fail conditions; strengthen only missing contracts/edges; build, verify, open an authorised PR and return evidence. |
| HeyStax | Shared operational substrate for scoped instructions, coding tasks, ownership/progress and results across people and agents. |
| Hooktunnel integration, planned | Faster handoff transport to qualify against the same identity, ownership, acknowledgement and audit contract; no new execution engine or permission source. |

The current research-led entry point is **issues → proportionate Specflow
strengthening → build → evidence returned**. Do not rerun discovery, compete with
the researcher's idea choice or require Colm to relay the task. A genuine missing
fact, contradiction or authority conflict becomes an evidenced needs-change
response to the research agent. A future general-intent/self-improvement entry
point may include candidate selection under its own registered policy, while
reusing the same downstream engine and gates.

## Replaceable roles, durable rules

The owner's live **Chief of Staff** Stax, read on 26 October, already defines a
portable role brief: duties include rule, trigger, output and
reason; changes must be recorded rather than made silently. This document does
not replace that brief, adopt its duties for every agent, or grant its permissions
to a coding worker. Project policies and frozen task contracts retain their scopes.
Resolve the configured private Stax reference at runtime; do not hard-code this
owner's Stax identifiers into the reusable kit.

Record role identity separately from its current holder. A successor reads the
authorised brief/revision, assignment, project intent, open issues, fixed contract,
task/run state and evidence. Preserve original authors and previous decisions.
Do not copy credentials, depend on a vendor's private memory, or silently grant
the successor its predecessor's access. Capability and current authority are checked
at the actual service boundary; a familiar display name is not authentication.

Replacement must revoke/fence the previous holder's protected operations and
reconcile unknown work before another authoritative worker starts. It must not
duplicate a PR, consumed holdout or experiment under a new agent/run name. A role
reassignment or new rule revision does not silently amend an already frozen
experiment. Apply rule changes through explicit revision/conflict handling.

The research role, coding role, chief of staff and notification transport can
each be replaced independently. Demonstrate this with real supported callers;
a portability statement or a fresh prompt is not sufficient proof.

## The complete loop

1. **Imagine:** explore valuable possibilities from the general intent.
2. **Constrain:** find hard walls using real numbers and dated, cited sources;
   keep a buildable, potentially monetizable hypothesis. Unknown data stays unknown.
3. **Pick:** choose the strongest allowed idea; explain alternatives, expected
   user/business value, uncertainty and selection. Colm does not rank every list.
4. **Contract:** fix the pass/fail rule before building. Select evidence for the
   actual question/risk; create issues and a branch within the permitted repo.
5. **Hand off:** post a structured task to the configured Stax with stable task
   and experiment IDs, contract revision, repo/branch, evidence and authority.
   `[CODING TASK]` is presentation, not an atomic claim or dispatch acknowledgement.
6. **Build:** one capable authorised worker obtains a fenced claim, builds with
   real data, runs the applicable checks and opens a PR when the policy permits.
   Duplicate watchers recover one job or an owned-task result, never two builds.
7. **Report and learn:** deliver verdict, evidence and next action to the issue
   and HeyStax. The researcher uses the authenticated result in the next report
   and idea choice. Reporting retries do not repeat the build.

The reported Modulo Lab policy permits `Hulupeep/modulo-lab` only, with no spend,
outside contact or other repos. That is an example registered scope, not a global
default or permission for this Specflow task. Neither task prose nor memory can
expand authority. No new merge/deploy authority follows from this mission.

## Verdicts and proportionate evidence

Keep **experiment verdict** (proven, disproven, needs a change) separate from the
existing **code decision** (KEEP, REVERT, INCONCLUSIVE); do not change its enum or
invent a one-to-one mapping. Proven needs the fixed conditions and adequate real
data. Disproven needs valid counter-evidence against the hypothesis. A broken
build, inaccessible fixture or invalid checker does not disprove the idea. Needs
a change names the missing evidence/failed assumption and the next experiment,
preserving the original rule and result. Passing build tests does not prove demand.

Use the lightest selector profile covering actual uncertainty and consequences:
regression/contracts for a known local defect; a real API/auth/persistence journey
for an integration; personas, kill check and actual journey for a new interaction;
a bounded real-input experiment for feasibility; observed product/market evidence
for usefulness or revenue. Label proxies. Data/auth/money risks retain their
applicable gates regardless of diff size. Reuse checks; do not add reviewers or
ceremony that cannot answer the uncertainty. Never weaken a frozen test after failure.

Choose bounded measurements that do not require routine owner judgement. If the
best route exceeds authority, choose an allowed alternative or finish with an
evidenced limitation and next action. Do not fabricate success or silently wait
for Colm. Unknown side effects remain fenced: no lock clearing, allowance reset
or holdout replay in the name of autonomy.

## HeyStax control and explanation

People and agents should set scoped intent, discover/claim work, inspect progress,
issue permitted controls and consume results through one project surface. Reuse
the existing execution service; the run ledger owns execution facts and HeyStax
owns project intent and presents those facts.

The selected command protocol must bind authenticated actor/scope and stable
command/task/run IDs, distinguish requested/acknowledged/applied/rejected/unknown,
and resolve conflicting instructions by explicit priority/revision rules. Saving
a Next Action is not proof of dispatch. Agent-authored approval is not an owner
grant. Execution completion and reporting delivery have separate states.

Transport speed is separate from delivery correctness. Qualify Hooktunnel's
actual capabilities before making reliability claims: duplicate, delayed,
out-of-order or lost notifications must reconcile to the same task/job, and
reconnect must not turn an acknowledged task into a new build. Each receipt links
the source issue/contract revision, sender, recipient, task, job and evidence.
Do not assume exactly-once delivery or treat a notification as an owner grant.

Explain what was chosen, why, alternatives, selected checks, actual execution,
what changed, both verdicts, exact limitation, remaining allowance and next action.
Link private evidence under scoped access; do not publish raw secrets/transcripts.

Observed MCP discovery on 26 October exposes project context, actions, typed
records and audit tools, but no run-dispatch, atomic claim or command-ack tool.
Do not infer these capabilities from a successful Stax text write. Reuse service
admission/fencing; qualify any new transport/schema at its owning slice.
